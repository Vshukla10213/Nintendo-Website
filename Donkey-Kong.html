Donkey Kong

The 1981 Arcade Origins: Saving Nintendo

In 1980, Nintendo of America was facing financial ruin after its shooter arcade game Radar Scope flopped,
leaving 2,000 unsold arcade cabinets sitting in a warehouse.
Nintendo president Hiroshi Yamauchi tasked an inexperienced staff artist, Shigeru Miyamoto, with designing a new game that could be retrofitted into those exact cabinets.



  • The Popeye Inception: Miyamoto initially wanted to create a game licensed from the Popeye comic strip.
    When licensing negotiations failed, he substituted the characters:
    Popeye became Jumpman (later renamed Mario), Olive Oyl became Pauline, and the brute Bluto became a gorilla.

  • The Name Myth: The name "Donkey Kong" was deliberately chosen by Miyamoto;
    "Donkey" was pulled from a Japanese-English dictionary to mean "stubborn," and "Kong" was common Japanese slang for a large ape.

  • The Universal Lawsuit: The arcade game was an immediate international phenomenon, prompting Universal Studios to sue Nintendo,
    claiming the game infringed on their King Kong intellectual property.
    Nintendo's lawyer, John Kirby, successfully argued that Universal had previously fought to declare King Kong in the public domain,
    winning the case and securing Nintendo's independent future.

Chronological Eras & Evolution

The Arcade Era (1981–1983)

Key Titles: Donkey Kong (1981), Donkey Kong Jr. (1982), Donkey Kong 3 (1983)

  • Pioneered the "platformer" genre.

  • DK Jr. features the only time in history where Mario acts as the main villain.

  • Funded Nintendo's ability to create the Famicom/NES.

The Rareware Golden Age (1994–1999)

Key Titles: Donkey Kong Country (1994), Diddy Kong Racing (1997), Donkey Kong 64 (1999)

  • Donkey Kong Country used groundbreaking pre-rendered 3D sprites on 16-bit hardware, selling 9.3 million copies.

  • Introduced Diddy Kong, Dixie Kong, and the villainous Kremlings.

  • Retconned the original arcade ape into Cranky Kong, making the modern DK his grandson.

The Experimental Era (2003–2005)

Key Titles: Donkey Konga (2003), Donkey Kong Jungle Beat (2004), Mario vs. Donkey Kong (2004)

  • Following Microsoft's purchase of Rare, Nintendo experimented with new genres.

  • Utilised unique peripherals like the DK Bongos for rhythm and platforming games.

  • Revived the original puzzle-style rivalry with Mario on handheld systems.

The Retro Studios Revival (2010–Present)

Key Titles: Donkey Kong Country Returns (2010), DKC: Tropical Freeze (2014), Donkey Kong Bananza (2025)

  • Returned the franchise to its brutal, precision-based 2D platforming roots.

  • The latest entry, Donkey Kong Bananza, reasserts the character's core role in Nintendo's modern hardware catalog.
